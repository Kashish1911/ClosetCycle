PROMPT FOR GEMINI

Create ONE complete, polished Business Model Canvas (BMC) diagram for Closet Cycle, a student-built campus clothing-exchange prototype. This must be a single integrated canvas diagram on one landscape page or one presentation slide. Do not create a multi-slide deck, separate cards, a Lean Canvas, a SWOT, or a PESTEL analysis.

CANVAS LAYOUT AND ARROWS

Use the standard 9-block Business Model Canvas arrangement: Key Partners on the far left; Key Activities above Key Resources to their right; Value Propositions in the center; Customer Relationships above Channels to the right; Customer Segments on the far right; and Cost Structure plus Revenue Streams as two wide blocks across the bottom. Label every block with its exact standard BMC heading.
Connect the nine blocks with clear, directional arrows so the diagram reads as one business system. Keep connectors outside text areas and avoid arrow crossings. Use this flow: Key Partners → Key Activities → Value Propositions → Customer Relationships → Channels → Customer Segments. Also connect Key Resources → Key Activities and Key Resources → Value Propositions. Use separate dashed financial arrows from Customer Segments and campus sponsors toward Revenue Streams; use cost arrows from Key Partners, Key Activities, and Key Resources toward Cost Structure. Include a small legend that says solid arrows = service/value flow, dashed gold arrows = possible revenue, and muted rust arrows = costs. These arrows illustrate relationships; do not imply existing contracts or paid users.

USE THIS CLOSET CYCLE CONTENT

KEY PARTNERS: Potential campus sustainability clubs, student societies, a college sustainability office, an approved payment gateway provider for a future live launch, and hosting/storage vendors. Mark all partnerships “proposed; none confirmed.”
KEY ACTIVITIES: Maintain the React/TypeScript website and Java 25 LTS/Spring Boot API; review listings and reports; support safe local handovers; build campus listing density; measure successful exchanges and user feedback; maintain privacy and security.
KEY RESOURCES: Closet Cycle web prototype; Java API and relational database; clothing listings and exchange workflow; image-storage capability; campus community access; moderator time; clear safety and privacy guidance. Cloud infrastructure is planned, not deployed.
VALUE PROPOSITIONS: Students can discover wearable pre-owned clothing near campus, list pieces they no longer use, request a direct swap or credit-balanced exchange, and confirm a handover together. Core browsing and exchanging remain free. Internal credits are prototype points only; they are not money.
CUSTOMER RELATIONSHIPS: Self-service browsing/listing; clear exchange status; simple safety guidance; reporting and moderator review; optional Pro support/features only if validated. Do not claim identity verification, guaranteed safety, or existing customer service operations.
CHANNELS: Proposed campus clubs and societies, opt-in clothing-swap events, student ambassadors, campus-approved social channels, and member referrals. These are go-to-market ideas, not launched channels.
CUSTOMER SEGMENTS: First segment: students and campus community members who want affordable clothing variety and have wearable clothes they no longer need. Potential second payer: a campus office or sponsor supporting an event. Demand is unvalidated; label segments as hypotheses.
REVENUE STREAMS: Clearly label “future experiments — no current revenue.” Hypothesis 1: optional Closet Cycle Pro at illustrative prices ₹49/month or ₹399/year, offering additional listing capacity, saved searches/match alerts, optional listing spotlight controls, and an exchange summary. Hypothesis 2: campus clothing-swap event sponsorship. Keep core exchanges free. The prototype displays a mock checkout with UPI, debit/credit cards, and net banking, but it does not collect payment data, connect to a gateway, or charge money. Payment methods are checkout choices, not separate revenue streams. Do not include selling credits, advertising claims, or a completed-exchange fee as approved revenue.
COST STRUCTURE: Frontend/API maintenance; planned cloud hosting, database, and image storage; domain; moderation and member support; campus outreach/events; accessibility and security work; and future payment-provider transaction fees/taxes if live payments are approved. No deployed AWS costs are being claimed.

VISUAL DIRECTION AND ACCURACY

Use Closet Cycle’s sustainable-fashion editorial style: warm cream background, forest green headings, muted sage blocks, small terracotta accent, refined serif title paired with clean sans-serif body type, generous whitespace, crisp thin borders, and simple clothing/community icons. Keep all labels readable at presentation size. Fit the entire canvas on one page without shrinking text too far. Make the connectors and legend visually clear.
Use no invented market-size figures, user counts, completed interviews, sales, customer testimonials, sponsors, partners, grants, environmental savings, or payment success. Distinguish implemented prototype features from proposed business hypotheses. Add a small footer: “Closet Cycle student prototype · proposed model for validation · no live payments or current revenue.” If the tool supports it, provide an editable vector or slide version and a high-resolution PNG export.
